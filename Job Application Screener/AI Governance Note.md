# AI Governance Notes — Project 2: Job Application Screener

**1. What decision does the AI make?**
The AI performs two distinct functions with different risk levels. First, it *extracts* facts from a CV (years of experience, education, key skills) — a factual-recovery task, reading a document and returning what it says rather than judging it. Second, it *scores* the candidate against that role's specific requirements, producing a 0–100 score, a tier, and stated reasoning per criterion. The AI does not decide who is hired. For Strong Fit and Consider tiers, it does not decide who advances to an interview either — that stays with HR. It does, however, directly determine two real outcomes without further human input: whether HR is proactively emailed about a candidate at all, and whether a candidate below 60 receives an automated rejection.

**2. What happens if it gets it wrong?**
An extraction error here (misreading years of experience, missing a qualification) is a serious risk in its own right, because it can change whether a candidate is ever seen by a human, not just a number in a ledger. A scoring error carries a distinct and equally serious risk: unfair or inconsistent treatment of applicants, particularly if the rubric's calibration by role — or its reading of the cover letter — carries any unintended pattern across candidates. The rejection-email path is the single highest-risk element in the whole workflow: a false negative here (a genuinely strong candidate scoring below 60 due to a CV misread or an unfairly harsh reasoning) results in that candidate being turned away with no human ever reviewing the decision.

**3. What's the human checkpoint?**
For Strong Fit candidates, HR is emailed and must independently decide on the interview — the AI's score is a recommendation, not an instruction, and the email states this explicitly. For Consider-tier candidates (60–74), there is no automated action at all beyond logging — the candidate simply awaits HR's own review, with no prompting either way. For candidates scoring below 60, there is **no human checkpoint before rejection**. This is a deliberate design decision, made explicitly rather than by default, and it represents the one point in this workflow where an automated action reaches someone outside the company with no review in between. This trade-off should be revisited periodically — for example, by sampling rejected applications and confirming the automated cutoff is not producing false negatives — rather than treated as a settled, low-risk default.

**4. What data does it touch, and what's the audit trail?**
Every candidate, regardless of tier, is logged — to `Shortlisted Applicants` or `Screened Applicants` — with the full score breakdown, per-criterion reasoning, and a timestamp. A `Recruiter Decision` field defaults to `"Pending review"` on every row and is never set by the AI; only a human changes it. The role-specific requirements the AI scores against are stored in a separate, HR-maintained sheet rather than hardcoded into the prompt, so the criteria being applied are visible and auditable independent of the workflow itself. The rejection email's wording was deliberately kept generic and does not quote the AI's specific reasoning back to the candidate, to avoid a rejected applicant receiving language that reads as an automated, possibly-contestable judgment of their qualifications.
